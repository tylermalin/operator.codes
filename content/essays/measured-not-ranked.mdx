## The click is no longer the unit

The first randomized test of Google's AI Overviews hid them from 1,065 US desktop users in early 2026. When an Overview appeared, organic clicks fell 39.8%. Satisfaction didn't change. More than 95% of participants never noticed their results were different.

Ahrefs, Pew and Seer measured the same effect with different methods and landed in the same direction. For planning, assume 40 to 60% fewer clicks on informational queries that trigger an AI answer. Treat any recovery as upside.

Demand didn't shrink. It moved. People now read and act on your content without ever visiting the page it came from.

## Most of what's being sold has no evidence behind it

The market responded the way markets do. Package the anxiety, sell a checklist. llms.txt files, AI-specific schema, "chunked" pages, and a rank tracker that reports a single AI position.

Here is what the evidence says so far:

- Server logs show 97% of llms.txt files get no requests from AI crawlers.
- The one controlled test of adding schema found no citation lift.
- Run the same brand-recommendation prompt twice and the two lists match less than once in 100 tries. A single "AI rank" is mostly noise.

Some of what's being sold is worse than useless. Microsoft's Defender team found more than 50 hidden prompt injections from 31 companies, many tucked into "Summarize with AI" buttons. That is being catalogued as an attack, not a growth tactic. Anything you wouldn't want a journalist to find in your page source is off limits.

## What actually moves a citation

The paper that named the field, the GEO study out of Princeton and collaborators, rewrote sources nine different ways and measured how much of an AI answer each one earned. Adding citations to the fifth-ranked page lifted its visibility 115%. The top-ranked page lost 30% under the same treatment.

Sourced claims beat keywords. Lower-ranked pages gained the most.

The off-site data points the same way. Across 75,000 brands, branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility about three times as strongly as backlinks. And a brand cited inside an Overview gets 120% more organic clicks per impression than one left out.

Read it as a pattern, not a trick list. Statistics and named sources lift visibility because they can be checked. Third-party mentions predict inclusion because they corroborate. First-hand content wins because the model doesn't already have it.

## The real issue is liability

Most people assume answer engines are a better ranking algorithm. They aren't. They're a different business with a different risk profile.

An engine that answers in its own voice carries the risk of being wrong. One academic measurement already finds about one claim in nine unsupported by the pages an Overview cites. Every operator of an answer engine has a reason to close that gap.

So the engine favors sources it can safely repeat. That's the whole mechanism, and everything in the evidence lines up behind it.

For twenty-five years, search rewarded the page that best matched a query and collected the most links. The engine pointed, and the user judged. Answer engines took on the judging. Judging requires evidence.

## What to do with it

Treat AI search as SEO plus reputation plus verification. Each layer depends on the one before it.

1. **Access.** Confirm in your logs that search and live-fetch bots get 200s, not 403s. CDN defaults have been quietly blocking AI crawlers on new domains. Blocking a training bot doesn't remove you from answers. Blocking a search bot does.
2. **Citability.** Make your most exposed pages the clearest, best-sourced statement of their facts. Lead with the claim. Attach a number, a date, or a named source. Never invent one.
3. **Corroboration.** Get referenced by credible third parties. That's where the strongest correlate lives.
4. **Measurement.** Track how often you appear across a fixed set of 50 to 200 buyer-intent prompts, run monthly. Position is noise. Frequency is the signal.

The coordination problem is the one that sinks most programs. Access belongs to engineering, citability to content, reputation to PR, measurement to analytics. When each team optimizes its own piece, the overall number doesn't move. One person has to own the presence number, with authority across all four.

## Truth is infrastructure

The missing layer in AI search is provenance: a reliable way for a machine to know where a claim came from, who stands behind it, and whether it held up.

It's the same gap I keep writing about in [carbon markets and physical-world data](/essays/truth-is-infrastructure), in [custody](/essays/custody-is-a-verification-problem), and in [agent liability](/essays/agent-can-sign). Systems that act on claims eventually have to price whether the claims are true.

Organizations that publish measured, sourced and corroborated facts are building that layer for their own category. At scale, the incentives now reward accuracy and shown work. Good institutions valued both long before search existed.
